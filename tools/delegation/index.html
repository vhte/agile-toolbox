How to use this

This board is built on Management 3.0 Delegation Poker (Jurgen Appelo): seven levels from Tell (the lead decides) to Delegate (the team decides). Each decision area also gets a second, independent setting for AI agents, because agents need their own calibration: an agent that can merge, deploy or email a customer is a new actor in your decision system.

  1. Start from a preset and delete what does not apply. Name decisions as verbs ("Merge a PR to main"), not topics ("Git").
  2. Play a poker round per contested row: everyone picks secretly, then reveal. A spread of 3 or more levels means people hold different pictures of who decides; talk about the extremes first.
  3. Write the guardrails and the rollback trigger before raising an agent level. Levels 4 and 5 let an agent act without approval, so the trigger ("one reverted change", "any customer complaint") must be concrete.
  4. Raise levels on evidence. A useful heuristic: if the human approver rejects or changes almost none of an agent's proposals for a decision, it is a candidate to move up one step, written as a rule with a rollback trigger.
  5. Publish the result. Export the AGENTS.md section (it starts at "## Autonomy and approvals") and paste it into your repository's AGENTS.md, for example under the Boundaries that the Working Agreements tool builds, so coding agents read the same rules people agreed on. Set a review date. A clear, communicated AI stance is the first capability in DORA's 2025 AI Capabilities Model.

Owners are roles, not people. Poker picks are never stored: only the agreed level and an anonymous count per level.

Make decision authority explicit for people and for AI agents.

List the decisions your team makes, agree who decides each one with Delegation Poker, and set how far AI agents may go on their own. Export it as a delegation matrix and as an AGENTS.md section for your repository.
